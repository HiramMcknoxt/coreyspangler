The Record — a Living Ledger

This page is a ledger, not a trophy case. Every entry carries a status, and statuses change when the evidence does: a pending ordinance that passes gets marked passed; a policy that stops working gets retired, here, in public. That is the Living Ledger applied to my own record.

Sourcing note: Cabot's minutes almost never print a sponsor line and never name the members voting "for," so sponsorship below is evidenced from the filed ordinances' sponsor blocks in council packets, my own legislative files, and my statements — and labeled where it rests on those. Last updated November 5, 2026.

Voting record

411 For · 5 Against · 2 Abstain — extracted from council minutes through the October 19, 2026 meeting. One caution that travels with these numbers: because the minutes never name the members voting "For," the For count is inferred from recorded outcomes; named Against and Abstain votes are recorded directly. I vote yes on running the city. My no votes cluster on what residents get charged, how far city power reaches into private property, who gets to serve, and how land-use rules get written.

Authored and sponsored legislation

Ordinance No. 33 of 2026 — No city funds for third-party ALPR systems

Pending Prohibits the appropriation or expenditure of city funds for automated license plate reader systems provided by third parties; honors existing contracts to expiration. The text is generic — the vendor's name never appears in it. I am the sponsor. First reading October 19, 2026; second reading November 2, 2026, where my motion to waive the third reading failed 4–4 (a waiver needs 6 of 8 under Ark. Code § 14-55-202(a)). Third reading: November 16, 2026; passage needs 5 of 8. It is not passed yet. See Synthesism as Praxis for the full account, and note what this ordinance is not: it does not authorize any city-run system, and I do not support one as the plan or the sequel.

Ordinance No. 21 of 2026 — AI religious exemption

Passed I wrote this ordinance. Adopted August 17, 2026, by unanimous roll call. No city employee can be compelled, as a condition of employment, to use generative or agentic AI that conflicts with sincerely held religious beliefs; a written First Amendment Technological Accommodation process is created, with standards unchanged and assistive technology expressly excluded. As far as anyone has been able to find, the first AI religious exemption law of its kind anywhere.

Ordinance No. 16 of 2026 — Water bill transparency (December sample bill)

Tabled Would have required the Cabot Water and Wastewater Commission to give every customer, each December, a sample bill projecting the next year's rates and listing all recurring fees. Filed with my name as sponsor. Tabled April 20, 2026 — on my own motion. A bill you can't explain is a bill you can't defend; I continue to work with the Commission on a public transparency process, and the ledger keeps this entry as tabled until the work lands.

Ordinance No. 20 of 2025 — Effective dates of ordinances

Passed Co-sponsored with Ed Long. Adopted June 16, 2025, unanimously, including the emergency clause. Establishes that an ordinance takes effect thirty days after passage and sets the referendum-petition deadline at thirty days, under A.C.A. § 14-55-203.

Ordinances No. 8, 9, and 10 of 2025 — Crypto-mine planning-district fix

Passed Adopted February 3, 2025, unanimously. Extended the Unified Development Code's coverage to the crypto mine site inside Cabot's planning district — the fix the area's state senator urged on the council on December 2, 2024. I sponsored the package and moved the adoption of No. 8. Later in 2025, the legislature passed Act 314, repealing municipal planning-district authority statewide; the same senator voted for the repeal. The local fix and its erasure are both in the ledger, because both are the record.

Ordinance No. 6 of 2025 — False alarms

Passed Co-sponsored with Stephen Redd (the filed sponsor block lists us both). Adopted January 27, 2025, unanimously. Amends the false-alarm article (Code § 16-84), assessing the costs of repeated false alarm calls on the Police Department.

Ordinance No. 2 of 2025 — Council organization and committees

Passed Adopted January 27, 2025, in amended form, unanimously. I wrote the committee-structure proposal ("Committee options," January 25, 2025; "For a New Committee Structure," January 26, 2025); at the meeting, Ed Long moved to replace the packet version with my plan, and the minutes record the substitution and adoption of the amended version.

Its most durable change is the §5 agenda rule: an item a committee advances out of a work session is placed on the agenda of a Regular Session on a subsequent date — same-date consideration only by a majority vote of the council. The public gets a window between "the committee liked it" and "the council passed it." One correction I insist on, including against my own earlier shorthand: the three-reading requirement and the two-thirds waiver standard are Arkansas law — Ark. Code § 14-55-202(a), on the books since 1875. I did not change the waiver threshold, and I don't claim it. My change is the §5 pause.

Resolution No. 49 of 2024 — Livestreaming council meetings

Passed Co-sponsored (with Mayor Ken Kincade, Brandon Hillenburg, Maggie Cope, Stephen Redd, and James Reid). Passed unanimously (8–0) on October 21, 2024. Formally authorizes livestreaming of city council meetings, with guidelines — the meetings stay public, on camera, with the public's comments open.

Party committee legislation

§601.4, Democratic Party of Lonoke County Bylaws — "Campaign Finance Limitations"

Passed Adopted January 23, 2026, unanimously; I authored it as county chair. The County Committee may designate entities "grossly misaligned" with the platform; the committee, its auxiliaries, and its candidates/nominees may not accept funds from designated entities; the Secretary maintains a public proscribed list; contributions are refunded within 30 days; the Rules Committee investigates violations. The adopted text contains no separate endorsement bar — the mechanism is the designation, and I state it the way it was adopted. Full account on the Praxis page.

Also on the record: an earlier state-level version of the same mechanism (the "Plebocracy" amendment to the Democratic Party of Arkansas rules) was pushed at the state party; its outcome is ambiguous in the record, and I label it unresolved rather than claim it.
