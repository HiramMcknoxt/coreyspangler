Meetings & Your Voice

Council business is the public's business. Here is how the meetings work, how the agenda reaches you before the vote, and how I ask for your position while it can still change mine.

How the council meets

The Cabot City Council does its work in Work Sessions — where items are presented, questioned, and shaped in committee — and Regular Sessions, where the votes happen. Agendas are published in advance of each meeting, and meetings are livestreamed with public comments open (Resolution No. 49 of 2024, which I co-sponsored, passed unanimously on October 21, 2024).

One rule I wrote into the council's own procedures matters here: under the §5 agenda rule (Ordinance No. 2 of 2025), an item a committee advances out of a work session goes on the agenda of a Regular Session on a subsequent date. Same-date consideration requires a majority vote of the council. The pause is the point: time for you to read the item, and for me to ask you about it, before anyone votes.

The agenda poll — weigh in before the vote

Before every regular meeting, I publish a poll covering the substantive items on the agenda: one question per item, written in plain English, with the item's own summary attached. After the meeting window closes, I publish the tallies.

What the poll is and isn't: it is an informal, self-selected read of the residents who chose to answer — useful input, honestly labeled. It is not a scientific survey, and I don't present it as one. It sits alongside what I hear at meetings, by email, and in the community; it does not replace judgment, and it never gets blended into claims about "what Cabot thinks" without its label attached.
